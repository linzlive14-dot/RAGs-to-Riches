WORKPLACE ACCOMMODATIONS POLICY

Synthetic training policy. It does not determine any real person's legal rights.

PURPOSE

The company provides reasonable workplace accommodations for qualified employees and applicants with disabilities, pregnancy-related limitations, sincerely held religious practices, and other needs covered by law. The process is individualized. A diagnosis, condition, or requested adjustment does not produce an automatic outcome.

REQUESTING AN ACCOMMODATION

A person does not need to use a special phrase or form. A statement that a medical condition, disability, pregnancy-related limitation, religious practice, or similar need is affecting application or work duties may start the process. Requests may be made to HR, a manager, recruiting, or the accommodations mailbox.

Managers must send the request to HR within one business day. They may ask what immediate work support is needed but must not request a diagnosis, medical history, genetic information, prescription list, or religious proof. Medical and supporting records are submitted through the confidential accommodations channel.

THE INTERACTIVE PROCESS

HR discusses the limitation, essential job functions, barriers, requested changes, alternatives, expected duration, and urgency. The employee and manager are expected to participate in good faith. The company may select an effective accommodation other than the employee's preferred option.

Possible accommodations include equipment, schedule changes, modified communication methods, accessible facilities, assistive technology, leave, reassignment of marginal tasks, remote work, or reassignment to a vacant role. Essential functions are not removed permanently unless required by law, but how or when a function is performed may change.

Remote work is assessed under this policy when connected to a covered need. Ordinary tenure requirements in the Remote and Hybrid Work Policy do not decide an accommodation request. HR still considers whether remote work is effective, whether essential functions can be performed, security, supervision, and other effective options.

DOCUMENTATION

When the limitation and need are not obvious, HR may request reasonable documentation from an appropriate provider or other source. The request is limited to information needed to understand the functional limitation, duration, and connection to an accommodation. A full medical file is not required.

Religious accommodation documentation is requested only when there is an objective reason to question either the religious nature or sincerity of a practice, and the inquiry remains narrow. HR considers that beliefs and observance may be unfamiliar or evolve over time.

The employee receives a reasonable deadline, normally 15 calendar days, and may explain delays. The company does not deny solely because a provider uses a different form if the supplied information is sufficient.

TEMPORARY MEASURES

When a decision requires time, HR considers temporary measures such as schedule flexibility, different equipment, remote work, leave, or task adjustments. A temporary measure is not a final determination and may be changed for effectiveness, safety, or undue hardship.

Managers may implement an obvious, low-risk adjustment while HR reviews it, but must document it with HR. Urgent safety or access needs receive priority.

DECISIONS

HR coordinates the decision with the employee, manager, Safety, Information Security, Legal, or other specialists as needed. Approval identifies the accommodation, effective date, responsible parties, duration or review date, and how problems should be reported.

A denial explains the reason in appropriate detail and identifies any alternative offered. Undue hardship is assessed using the applicable legal standard and company resources, not solely a team's preference or budget. Safety decisions rely on objective evidence and an individualized assessment.

CONFIDENTIALITY

Accommodation and medical records are stored separately from ordinary personnel records. Managers receive only the restrictions, approved adjustments, dates, and implementation details they need. Coworkers are not told an employee's medical or religious information. Managers may explain only that workplace arrangements are handled consistently with company process.

LEAVE AND RETURN

Leave may be an accommodation even when ordinary paid time or statutory leave is exhausted. HR assesses expected duration, operational impact, and alternatives. Indefinite leave with no reasonably predictable return may not be reasonable, but labels are not used in place of an individualized review.

Before return, HR may request information about work restrictions when lawful and relevant. A return with restrictions starts or continues the interactive process. Employees must not be forced to remain out merely because they are not at full capacity if an effective accommodation permits work.

REVIEW AND CHANGES

Accommodations are reviewed on the stated date and whenever duties, condition, location, technology, or effectiveness materially changes. Employees should report an ineffective accommodation promptly. A manager must consult HR before changing or ending an accommodation.

NO RETALIATION

Retaliation, interference, coercion, and disclosure of confidential accommodation information are prohibited. Concerns may be reported to HR, the ethics line, or a government agency. Participation in the process does not excuse unrelated performance standards, but standards must be applied with approved accommodations in place.

PREGNANCY, LACTATION, AND RELIGION

Pregnancy, childbirth, and related medical needs can be the basis for an accommodation. Examples include a closer parking space, a seat, extra restroom breaks, a lifting limit, time for prenatal care, and a temporary change to travel. The employee does not need a particular form of words. Telling HR or a manager that a change is needed because of pregnancy is enough to start the conversation. Medical details go to HR, not to the whole team.

Break time and a private space to express milk are provided as described in the workplace logistics policy. Those arrangements should be made without requiring the employee to argue for them. If the published room does not work, HR finds another private space that is not a restroom.

Religious accommodations include schedule changes for observance, exceptions to the dress standard, and a quiet place to pray when one can be offered without undue hardship. HR asks what workplace change is needed, not for proof of membership in a congregation. A coworker who wants a different religious accommodation uses the same process. Managers do not rank religions or grant a change to one employee and refuse a similar change to another without an operational reason reviewed by HR.
