# Performance, Learning, and Separation Policy

> Synthetic training policy. Ratings, bonuses, and tuition amounts are fictional demonstration figures.

## Performance cycle

RAGs to Riches Demo Company runs a formal performance review once a year, with self-assessments opening on the first Monday of February and manager reviews due by the last Friday of March. A lighter mid-year check-in happens in September. The check-in is a conversation and does not by itself change pay. New hires who start on or after October 1 receive onboarding goals and a 90-day conversation instead of the full annual review for that cycle.

Each review uses four ratings: Exceeds Expectations, Meets Expectations, Developing, and Does Not Meet Expectations. The rating compares results and behavior to the goals and to the role's expectations for that period. It is not a ranking against teammates. A manager shares the written review with the employee in a meeting before the rating is final in the HR system. The employee may add comments within 10 business days. Comments are stored with the review and do not automatically change the rating.

## Goals and feedback

Managers and employees set three to five goals at the start of the review year. At least one goal should describe how the work is done, such as collaboration or customer care, and the others should describe outcomes. Goals can be revised when the role or the business priority changes. A revision is noted in the HR system so the year-end review uses the goals that actually applied.

Feedback should be specific and timely. Managers are expected to mention both progress and concerns during the year, not only at the annual review. An employee may ask HR to join a review meeting when they believe the rating does not match the written examples. HR looks at the goals, the examples, and whether the same standard was used for similar roles. HR does not promise a particular rating.

## Performance improvement

When performance is below expectations and coaching has not closed the gap, the manager and HR may start a performance improvement plan. The plan states the gap, the expected result, the support the company will provide, and a review date, normally 30 or 60 calendar days. The employee receives a copy and may respond in writing. During the plan, previously approved remote or hybrid work may be adjusted if in-person coaching is part of the support, using the notice rules in the remote-work policy.

A plan is not discipline by itself, and completing it successfully returns the employee to the ordinary review cycle. If the gap remains, HR and the manager decide on a further plan, a role change, or separation. Serious misconduct is handled under the conduct policy and does not require a performance plan first.

## Pay changes and bonuses

Base-pay recommendations follow the annual review and are effective on the first payday in May when the budget is approved. A Meets Expectations rating is eligible for a standard increase. An Exceeds Expectations rating is eligible for a larger increase. A Developing or Does Not Meet rating is generally not eligible that cycle. An increase is not guaranteed. Off-cycle increases require Head of People approval and a documented reason such as a promotion or a correction of an internal inequity.

The annual bonus, when funded, is discretionary and is paid in May for the prior calendar year to employees who are active on the payout date and who were hired before October 1 of the bonus year. The target is stated in the offer letter. The company may reduce or skip the bonus in a year when it is not funded. A bonus is not wages for hours already worked unless a written plan says it is.

## Tuition assistance

After 12 months of continuous employment, a regular employee scheduled for 30 or more hours per week may request tuition assistance of up to $5,250 per calendar year. The course must relate to the current role or to a role the company reasonably expects the employee to hold. The employee submits the request before the course starts. HR and the manager approve the school, the course, and the amount.

Assistance is reimbursed after the employee submits a grade of B or better, or Pass in a pass/fail course, and an itemized receipt. Courses that are dropped, failed, or graded below B are not reimbursed. If the employee leaves the company within 12 months after a reimbursement, the employee repays the amount paid in that 12-month window, except when the location prohibits repayment or the separation is a company layoff. Repayment is arranged with Payroll and is not taken from wages unless the employee authorizes it.

## Referrals and professional memberships

An employee may refer a candidate through the careers portal. If the candidate is hired into a regular role and is still employed after 90 days, the referring employee receives a $1,500 referral payment on the next payday, subject to taxes. The referring employee must be active on the payout date and must not be the hiring manager or the interviewer who made the selection decision. Executives and members of the recruiting team are not eligible. More than one employee cannot split a referral for the same candidate; the first complete portal submission controls.

The company reimburses up to $300 per year for a professional membership or a job-related license renewal that the role requires or that the manager approves in advance. Receipts follow the expense-report timing in the travel and expense policy.

## Resignation and notice

An employee who resigns from an individual-contributor role is asked to give at least two weeks of written notice. A people manager is asked to give at least four weeks. Notice is given to the manager and to HR. The company may accept the notice, ask the employee to work some or all of it, or release the employee earlier and pay only for days worked plus any required payout. Paid time off is not normally scheduled during a notice period except for a day that was approved before the resignation.

The last day of work is the last day the employee performs duties. It is not extended by unused paid time off unless HR approves that in writing. Employees should return badges, keys, and company equipment before leaving on the last day, or ship them within five business days if they work remotely. Shipping instructions come from Workplace Experience. Access to company systems ends at the close of the last day.

## Job abandonment and references after exit

If an employee is absent for three consecutive scheduled workdays without notice, and does not respond to a documented attempt to reach them, HR may treat the employment as ended for job abandonment. HR still attempts contact before the record is closed. An emergency that prevented notice is considered if the employee explains it promptly after they are able to do so.

After separation, official verifications confirm only dates and title, and pay if the former employee authorizes it. A manager who wants to give a personal recommendation does so as an individual, without company letterhead, and without sharing performance-review documents or investigation records. Rehire is allowed when the employee left in good standing, meaning notice was given or waived, equipment was returned, and there was no serious misconduct finding. A hiring manager who wants to rehire someone within 12 months asks HR to review the file first.

## Onboarding and the introductory period

A new employee's first week is coordinated by the manager and People Operations. Before the start date, the employee completes tax forms, direct deposit, an identity check, and a background check appropriate to the role. A background check looks at identity and the checks listed in the offer. It does not ask neighbors or family for opinions. If a check returns information that might change the hiring decision, HR talks with the candidate and follows the notice process required for that location before making a decision. Employment can be withdrawn only after that process.

The first 90 calendar days are an introductory period used for training, equipment, access, and early feedback. The introductory period is not a promise of employment for 90 days, and it does not change at-will status where at-will employment applies. At about 30, 60, and 90 days the manager holds a short check-in. Goals for this period are smaller than annual goals: learn the tools, meet the team, and complete one assigned piece of work. Paid time off accrues from the hire date, but planned vacation during the first 90 days needs manager approval and is usually limited to days that were agreed in the offer.

Company equipment for a new hire, including a laptop and a badge, is requested by the manager at least five business days before the start date. Remote hires receive shipping instructions. Access to email is turned on by the start date when the background check is complete. If a check is still open, the start date moves rather than granting access early.

## Internal transfers

An employee who has completed the introductory period and is not on a performance improvement plan may apply for a posted internal role. The employee tells the current manager before interviewing, or at the same time the application is submitted if the conversation cannot happen first. Recruiting tells the current manager once an interview is scheduled. An internal move requires the receiving manager, the current manager, and HR to agree on a transfer date, normally two to four weeks out. Pay for the new role is set with the new offer, not by the employee keeping the old pay automatically. A transfer does not reset the hire date, paid time off balance, or retirement vesting.
