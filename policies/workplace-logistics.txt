WORKPLACE LOGISTICS POLICY

Synthetic training policy. Offices, rooms, and contacts described here are fictional.

STANDARD HOURS AND CORE TIME

The usual on-site schedule is 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. in the local time of the assigned office, with a meal period. Teams may set a different customer-coverage schedule in writing. Core collaboration time is 10:00 a.m. to 3:00 p.m. in the team's primary time zone. During core time, employees are reachable on the team channel unless they are in a meeting, on a break, or on approved time away. Starting earlier or later is fine when the manager agrees and customer coverage is met. A permanent change to the scheduled hours is recorded in the HR system so payroll and paid time off use the right schedule.

DRESS AND APPEARANCE

The everyday dress standard is business casual. Clothing should be clean, intact, and appropriate for the work and for any customer the employee will meet that day. A client meeting, an executive briefing, or a recorded external presentation calls for business attire unless the host says otherwise. Roles in a lab, warehouse, or facilities area follow the posted safety standard, including closed-toe shoes and any required protective equipment. Those items that the company requires are provided by the company.

Headphones are welcome in open offices. Clothing with a violent, harassing, or discriminatory message is not. A religious garment or a grooming practice related to religion, culture, or disability is welcome. If a safety rule conflicts with that practice, the manager contacts HR for an accommodation review before insisting on a change. Managers give dress feedback in private.

BADGES, VISITORS, AND DELIVERIES

Employees wear a visible company badge in offices that issue badges. A lost badge is reported the same day to Workplace Experience so it can be deactivated. A temporary badge is available at reception with a photo identification check. Visitors sign in, wear a visitor badge, and stay with a host. A visitor does not receive a badge that opens restricted rooms. Employees do not hold a secure door for someone they cannot identify.

Personal packages may be delivered to the office, but the company is not responsible for them and may ask an employee to stop high-volume personal deliveries. Suspicious or unexpected packages are left unopened and reported to reception or security. Company equipment shipments are accepted by Workplace Experience and logged before they go to a desk.

PARKING AND TRANSIT

Office parking, where it exists, is first-come and is not reserved except for marked accessible spaces and a small set of visitor spaces. Employees do not park overnight unless they are approved to travel the next morning and Workplace Experience has agreed. The company does not pay parking tickets or towing charges for a personal vehicle. A transit or parking subsidy, if offered at that office, is described in the office addendum and is requested in the HR system during enrollment. Commuting between home and the assigned office is not a business expense.

LACTATION AND WELLNESS ROOMS

Each staffed office has a lactation room that is private, shielded from view, and free from intrusion. It is not a restroom. A nursing employee may reserve it on the office calendar and may take reasonable paid breaks to express milk for one year after the child's birth, or longer if a location requires it. The room includes a chair, a surface, and access to a nearby refrigerator. Expressed milk may be stored in that refrigerator in a labeled personal container. Employees who work remotely make their own private arrangements and are still entitled to the break time.

A wellness room, when the office has one, may be used for a short rest, prayer, or a medical need. It is not a private office and reservations are limited to 30 minutes when others are waiting.

INJURIES, ILLNESS AT WORK, AND EMERGENCIES

An employee who is injured at work, or who becomes ill and needs help, tells the manager and contacts the local emergency number if the situation is urgent. Workplace injuries are also reported in the safety portal before the end of the day, or as soon as the employee is able. The report is used for care and prevention. It is not a conduct write-up. A coworker who sees a hazard reports it even if no one was hurt.

Emergency procedures are posted by the elevator and in the office wiki. Employees participate in drills. During a drill or a real evacuation, employees leave by the stairs when instructed and meet at the posted assembly point. Remote employees follow the local emergency guidance for their home and tell the manager when they are safe if a regional event disrupts work.

SHARED SPACES AND PERSONAL ITEMS

Kitchens and pantries are shared. Employees clean their own dishes and label food they put in the refrigerator. Unlabeled food may be discarded on Friday afternoons. The company does not replace personal dishes, food, or small appliances that an employee chooses to leave at the office, and it does not cover loss or damage to those personal items.

Desk items should leave aisles clear. In an office that uses shared desks, the employee clears the surface at the end of the day and stores personal items in the assigned locker. Service animals are welcome. Other animals are not brought to the office. The workplace is smoke-free, including vaping, within the building and within 25 feet of an entrance.

SUPPLIES, SEATING, AND FACILITIES REQUESTS

Everyday office supplies are available in the supply area. Specialized items, a replacement chair, a monitor arm, or a seating change are requested in the Workplace Experience portal. A request tied to pain, disability, or pregnancy is also sent to HR so it can be handled as an accommodation when that is appropriate. Facilities aims to acknowledge a portal request within two business days. Temperature, lighting, noise, and cleaning concerns go to the same portal. A manager does not need to approve a routine supply request.

Employees who split time between home and the office reserve a shared desk for on-site days when the office uses a reservation system. A reservation that is no longer needed should be released so someone else can use the seat. Repeated no-shows may cause the reservation to be opened after 30 minutes.
