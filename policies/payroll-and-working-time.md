# Payroll and Working Time Policy

> Synthetic training policy. Pay dates, rates, and examples are fictional and are not a promise of wages.

## Pay schedule and pay stubs

RAGs to Riches Demo Company pays employees every other Friday. If a payday falls on a company holiday, pay is issued on the preceding business day. A new hire is included in the first payday that falls at least five business days after Payroll receives a complete direct-deposit authorization and tax forms. Until then, pay may be issued by pay card.

Each payday the employee can view a pay stub in the HR system. The stub shows hours, gross pay, taxes, benefit deductions, paid time off used, and net pay. Employees should review the stub within two pay periods and tell Payroll about a missing payment, a wrong rate, or a deduction they do not recognize. Payroll corrects a confirmed company error on the next practical payday, or sooner when the error creates a hardship.

## Direct deposit and tax forms

Pay is delivered by direct deposit to one or two accounts at a United States bank, or to the company pay card. The employee enters account changes in the HR system. A change made fewer than five business days before payday takes effect on the following payday. Payroll will not change an account based on an email or a chat message, because those channels are used in fraud attempts. The employee must use the signed-in HR system.

Employees submit withholding forms during onboarding and may update them at any time. Payroll applies the form that is active at the start of the pay period. The company does not give tax advice. Questions about how much to withhold should go to a tax adviser. Year-end wage statements are available in the HR system by January 31 for the prior calendar year.

## Exempt and non-exempt status

Human Resources classifies each role as exempt or non-exempt under the rules that apply to the work location. The offer letter states the classification. A job title alone does not decide it. Non-exempt employees record all hours worked and are eligible for overtime. Exempt employees are paid on a salary basis for the workweek and do not receive overtime, except where a location expressly requires it.

An employee who believes the classification is wrong should contact HR. Raising that question in good faith is not a conduct issue. Managers must not ask a non-exempt employee to work without recording the time.

## Workweeks, overtime, and preapproval

The workweek begins at 12:00 a.m. Monday and ends at 11:59 p.m. Sunday in the employee's primary work-location time zone. Non-exempt employees receive one and one-half times the regular rate for hours worked over 40 in the workweek, unless the location requires a more favorable daily overtime rule. Paid time off, holidays, and bereavement hours do not count as hours worked for overtime.

Non-exempt overtime should be approved by the manager before the work is performed, except for an urgent customer or safety need. Working unapproved overtime does not forfeit pay that was earned. Repeated unapproved overtime is a scheduling conversation, not a reason to withhold wages. Exempt employees are expected to complete the role's work and may flex hours within the week with the manager's knowledge. An exempt employee who works an unusually heavy week is not paid extra, but the manager should rebalance the workload.

## Timesheets

Non-exempt employees submit a timesheet every Monday by 10:00 a.m. local time for the prior workweek. The timesheet includes start and stop times and unpaid meal periods. Exempt employees do not submit a daily timesheet, but they do record full-day absences in the HR system. A manager approves timesheets by Tuesday at noon. If the manager is out, the skip-level manager approves them so pay is not delayed.

Employees may not complete another person's timesheet. Falsifying time, including recording time while not working or omitting time that was worked, is a serious conduct issue and can also be a wage violation. Corrections are made in the HR system with a short note.

## Meal periods and rest breaks

A non-exempt employee who works more than six hours in a day takes an unpaid meal period of at least 30 minutes, starting no later than the end of the fifth hour when the location requires that timing. The meal period is duty-free. If the employee is required to stay available or to eat at the workstation, the meal period is paid and the employee records it as time worked.

Non-exempt employees also receive a paid rest break of 10 minutes for each four hours worked, or more when local law requires it. Breaks should be taken near the middle of the work period when practical. They are not saved up to leave early and are not deducted from paid time off. Exempt employees should take a reasonable meal break. Skipping meals is not a performance expectation.

Nursing employees may take reasonable paid break time to express milk. The break may run longer than the standard rest break. See the workplace logistics policy for the private room.

## On-call time

A non-exempt employee who must remain available and able to respond within 15 minutes is recording on-call hours as hours worked, including overnight restrictions that prevent ordinary personal activity. A requirement only to carry a phone and call back within one hour, while otherwise free, is not hours worked until the employee actually responds. Response time is always paid. On-call expectations must be written in the team rota. They are not created by an informal chat message after hours.

## Payroll deductions and garnishments

Voluntary deductions, such as benefit premiums and retirement contributions, appear on the pay stub and can be changed only through the enrollment or retirement channel. Involuntary deductions, such as tax levies and child-support orders, are applied when Payroll receives a valid order. The employee receives a copy of the order notice that Payroll is allowed to share. Coworkers and managers do not receive garnishment details.

The company does not deduct for missing equipment, cash shortages, or damaged property from wages unless the employee has given a written authorization that the location allows, or a law requires the deduction. A repayment plan is handled by Payroll, not by a manager withholding hours.

## Employment verification and final pay

Outside parties who want to verify employment contact People Operations at verify@example.invalid or the verification portal linked from the HR system. The company confirms dates of employment and job title. It confirms pay only when the employee has authorized that release in the portal. Managers must not write pay or performance details in a reference email. A personal reference that a coworker chooses to give must not use company letterhead or imply an official statement.

When employment ends, final wages are paid on the next regular payday, or sooner when the work location requires it. The final stub includes wages through the last day worked and any paid time off that the location requires the company to pay out. Unused floating holidays are not paid out. Employees should update their mailing address before the last day so year-end forms arrive.

## Wage statements, addresses, and name changes

Year-end wage statements are posted in the HR system and are not mailed unless the employee asks Payroll in writing. An employee who needs a copy of an older statement can download it for the years the system still shows, which in this demo is the current year and the two prior years. Address changes are made by the employee in the HR system and apply to tax forms, benefits mail, and final pay. A manager cannot submit an address change for someone else.

A legal name change is requested through HR with the document the location accepts, such as a court order or an updated identity document. Preferred names and pronouns can be updated in the HR system without those documents and are what coworkers and the chat assistant should use. Payroll uses the legal name on tax forms until the legal-name request is complete. Pay is not delayed because a preferred-name change is pending.

## Schedule changes and weekend work

A manager who changes a non-exempt employee's scheduled start time, end time, or scheduled day gives at least seven calendar days' notice, unless the employee agrees to a shorter notice or an urgent coverage need makes that impossible. The new schedule is written in the HR system. A one-time shift swap between two employees is allowed when both people agree, both are trained for the work, and the manager approves it before the shift. Swaps do not move overtime from one person to another on paper if the hours were actually worked by someone else.

Weekend coverage is posted on a rota. Non-exempt employees record those hours on the timesheet, and the hours count toward the 40-hour overtime threshold. Exempt employees who cover a weekend should take equivalent time away during the same or the following workweek when the business can support it. The company does not treat weekend messages as a requirement to work unless the employee is on the published on-call rota or the manager assigns the work.
