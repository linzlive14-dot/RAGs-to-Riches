# Leaves of Absence Policy

> Synthetic training policy. HR must evaluate actual leave rights by location and circumstances.

## General principles

The company provides job-protected and discretionary leaves for qualifying medical, family, parental, military, civic, safety, and personal needs. Employees are not expected to identify the correct legal program. A statement that time away may involve a serious health condition, caregiving, pregnancy, military duty, or personal safety must be referred to HR for confidential review.

Managers must not request a diagnosis, discourage a request, promise approval, or count potentially protected time under ordinary attendance rules before HR reviews it. Employees may contact HR directly. Retaliation for requesting or using protected leave is prohibited.

## Notice and intake

For foreseeable leave, an employee should contact HR at least 30 calendar days before the expected start. If 30 days is not practical, notice must be given as soon as practical. For an emergency, the employee or representative may notify the manager first, and the manager must alert HR within one business day.

The initial notice needs only the expected start date, estimated duration or intermittent schedule, contact information, and enough nonmedical context for HR to identify possible coverage. Medical certification and other sensitive records go to the secure leave administrator, not to the manager.

HR sends eligibility, rights, responsibilities, and certification notices within the applicable legal timeframe. The employee must return requested certification by the stated deadline, normally 15 calendar days, or explain diligent efforts and a need for additional time.

## Medical and family leave

Employees who meet service, hours, worksite, and reason requirements may qualify for protected family and medical leave. The synthetic company uses the calendar-year method for internal planning, but the legally required method for the employee's location controls. HR determines eligibility as of the leave start.

Qualifying reasons can include the employee's serious health condition; care for a spouse, child, parent, or other person covered by local law; birth and bonding; adoption or foster placement; military exigency; or military caregiver needs. Leave may be continuous, reduced-schedule, or intermittent when medically necessary or otherwise required.

Employees must make a reasonable effort to schedule foreseeable treatment to reduce disruption, subject to the provider's direction. A manager may coordinate work handoffs but may not require work during protected leave.

## Parental leave

Eligible regular employees receive up to six weeks of company-paid parental bonding leave after birth, adoption, or foster placement. The employee must have completed six months of employment by the event date. Paid parental leave runs concurrently with any available job-protected bonding leave and must be used within 12 months after placement or birth.

Birth parents may also qualify for medical recovery benefits. Bonding leave is available without regard to gender or caregiver label. If both parents work for the company, each receives the company-paid benefit, while statutory limits are applied as required by law.

## Pay and benefits

Job protection and pay are separate. A leave may be protected but unpaid. Available statutory payments, short-term disability, company parental pay, PTO, and unpaid time are coordinated to avoid more than 100 percent of normal base pay.

Health coverage continues during protected leave on the same terms as active employment while the employee pays the normal contribution. HR supplies payment instructions for unpaid periods. Failure to pay after required notice may end coverage, subject to reinstatement and continuation rights.

PTO accrual follows the Paid Time Off Policy. Holidays during leave are paid only when the applicable pay program or law provides payment. Bonus and commission treatment follows the written incentive plan.

## Communication during leave

HR or the leave administrator may request periodic status and return-date updates that are lawful and reasonable. The manager may send essential workplace announcements but should not request routine deliverables, monitor medical details, or pressure an early return.

Employees must report a material change in duration or schedule as soon as practical. Suspected misuse is referred to HR and investigated without surveillance that interferes with protected rights.

## Return to work

When permitted, HR may require a fitness-for-duty certification addressing the essential job functions identified in advance. HR, not the manager, receives the certification. The employee is returned to the same or an equivalent role when required.

If restrictions remain, HR begins the interactive accommodation process. An employee must not return to safety-sensitive work contrary to documented restrictions. If no protected leave remains, HR assesses accommodation, workers' compensation, and discretionary leave before separation is considered.

## Personal leave

An employee with at least one year of service may request an unpaid personal leave of up to 30 calendar days for a substantial personal need not covered elsewhere. Approval depends on staffing, performance, prior leave, and business impact. Personal leave is not job-protected unless a law applies, and a comparable opening rather than the same role may be offered at return.

The Head of People must approve extensions. Personal leave cannot be used to avoid performance management, work for a competitor, or extend an unauthorized absence.

## Escalation and records

HR maintains leave records separately from ordinary personnel files. Managers receive only dates, schedule limits, work restrictions, and coordination information they need. Any complaint of interference, retaliation, confidentiality failure, or inconsistent treatment is escalated to the Head of People and Legal.
