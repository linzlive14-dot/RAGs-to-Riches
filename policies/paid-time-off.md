# Paid Time Off and Attendance Policy

> Synthetic training policy. Balances and examples are fictional.

## Covered employees

Regular full-time and part-time employees scheduled for at least 20 hours per week receive paid time off under this policy. Interns, temporary agency workers, and independent contractors do not accrue company PTO unless a written agreement or local law says otherwise. Union-represented employees follow the applicable agreement when it differs.

PTO is intended for vacation, personal matters, short-term illness, and appointments. Statutory sick or safe leave is tracked separately where required. Employees are never required to use PTO for protected leave when the law prohibits that requirement.

## Accrual schedule

Eligible employees accrue PTO each pay period from the hire date. Full-time employees with less than three completed years of service accrue 15 days per year. At three completed years, the rate becomes 20 days per year beginning with the next full pay period. At seven completed years, the rate becomes 25 days per year. Part-time accrual is prorated according to scheduled weekly hours.

Accrual pauses during an unpaid leave after the first 30 consecutive calendar days unless law or a written leave approval provides otherwise. Accrual resumes when the employee returns to paid status. Employees do not accrue beyond the applicable cap: 1.5 times the annual accrual amount. Once below the cap, accrual restarts prospectively; missed accrual is not restored.

The HR system is the record of balance. A displayed balance may include time requested but not yet approved. Employees should review the transaction detail and contact HR if a pay period is missing or an accrual rate appears incorrect.

## Requesting planned time

Employees request planned PTO in the HR system before making nonrefundable commitments. Requests of one or two scheduled workdays should normally be submitted at least five calendar days in advance. Requests of three through five workdays should be submitted at least 10 calendar days in advance. Requests longer than five workdays should be submitted at least 30 calendar days in advance.

The manager approves requests based on available balance, minimum staffing, deadlines, fairness among overlapping requests, and the order in which complete requests were received. A request is not approved until the HR system shows approval. Managers should not deny statutory sick leave or protected leave under ordinary vacation criteria.

When two vacation requests conflict, the manager first looks for voluntary schedule changes. If coverage remains insufficient, the manager considers business impact, prior approvals, and equitable rotation of high-demand dates. Seniority alone does not control. A manager should explain a denial and, where practical, identify alternative dates.

## Unplanned absence

An employee who cannot report to work must notify the manager through the team's designated channel as soon as practical and preferably at least one hour before the scheduled start. The notice should state that the employee will be absent, the expected duration if known, and urgent handoff needs. Employees do not need to provide a diagnosis to the manager.

For three or more consecutive scheduled workdays of illness, HR may request documentation allowed by local law and will assess whether a medical or protected leave process applies. Patterns suggesting misuse may be reviewed, but no adverse decision may be based on protected activity. Emergencies and inaccessible communication are considered before attendance action.

## Using and recording PTO

PTO is recorded in quarter-hour increments for non-exempt employees and half-day increments for exempt employees, unless local law requires a smaller increment. PTO cannot be used for hours actually worked. Non-exempt employees may not perform off-the-clock work during PTO.

Employees may request up to five days of negative PTO only for a first-year planned absence and only with manager and HR approval. Negative balances are not available during a resignation notice period. If employment ends with a negative balance, recovery from final pay occurs only when lawful and authorized.

Company holidays do not reduce PTO when the holiday falls on a day the employee was otherwise scheduled to work. If an employee becomes ill during approved vacation, conversion to statutory sick leave depends on local rules and timely documentation.

## Carryover and year end

At the end of the calendar year, up to five unused PTO days carry into the next year unless the employee's work location requires a larger amount. Carried days are used before current-year accrual and expire on March 31 unless expiration is prohibited. HR publishes location-specific exceptions before annual enrollment.

Managers must not pressure employees to forfeit PTO or work while recording PTO. Teams should plan coverage throughout the year. Employees approaching the accrual cap receive a system notice, but failure to receive a notice does not change the cap.

## Separation and payout

Accrued unused PTO is paid at separation only when required by the employee's work location or a written employment agreement. The synthetic location register identifies the general payout rule, but HR must confirm the rule at the separation date. Unapproved future requests do not reduce the payable accrued balance.

Employees may not extend employment by placing PTO after their final active day unless HR approves an exception. Suspected errors in final PTO calculations should be submitted to HR within 30 calendar days; statutory rights remain unchanged.

## Protected leave and escalation

PTO approval is separate from job-protected leave. Employees needing time for their own serious health condition, caregiving, parental bonding, military duties, jury service, bereavement, voting, or safety-related reasons should review the applicable leave policy or contact HR. The company will coordinate balances without requiring an employee to disclose private medical facts to a manager.

Questions involving local law, a possible accommodation, retaliation, or inconsistent treatment must be escalated to HR. This policy is guidance and does not override more protective law.
