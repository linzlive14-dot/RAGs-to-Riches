# Health and Welfare Benefits Policy

> Synthetic training policy. It is not a plan document or a promise of coverage.

## Eligibility

Regular employees scheduled for 30 or more hours per week are eligible for medical, dental, and vision coverage. Regular employees scheduled for 20 to 29 hours are eligible for the employee assistance program and voluntary benefits identified during enrollment. Temporary workers and contractors are not eligible for company plans unless a written plan document expressly includes them.

Newly eligible employees may enroll beginning on the hire date. Coverage begins on the first day of the month following 30 calendar days of eligible employment. If the hire date is the first of a month, coverage still begins on the first day of the following month. The plan document controls if it provides a more favorable effective date.

Eligible dependents generally include a legal spouse or recognized domestic partner and children through the end of the month in which they turn 26. Documentation may be required and is reviewed confidentially. A dependent who is disabled may qualify beyond age 26 under plan terms.

## Enrollment

Employees must complete initial enrollment within 30 calendar days after the hire date. If no election is made, the employee receives only employer-paid default benefits listed in the enrollment guide and must wait for annual enrollment or a qualifying life event to add optional coverage.

Annual enrollment occurs during the period announced by Benefits, normally in October. Elections take effect January 1. Employees should review beneficiaries, covered dependents, network availability, payroll contributions, and health account elections each year. Prior elections may roll forward except flexible spending account amounts, which require a new election.

Enrollment confirmation in the benefits portal is the employee's receipt. Employees should retain it and report an incorrect election within 10 calendar days. Benefits will correct administrative errors when supported by timely evidence and plan rules.

## Qualifying life events

Marriage, divorce, birth, adoption, loss or gain of other coverage, and certain changes in employment or residence may permit a midyear election. The employee must notify Benefits and submit required documentation within 30 calendar days of the event. Birth and adoption changes may have a longer period under a controlling plan or law.

The requested election must be consistent with the event. For example, gaining a spouse may permit adding the spouse, while loss of outside coverage may permit enrolling affected family members. Benefits determines the permitted change under the plan and applicable tax rules.

Employees should not email medical records or identity documents to a manager. Documents must be uploaded through the secure benefits portal or another approved channel.

## Costs and payroll

The company and employee share premium costs according to the annual contribution schedule. Employee contributions are normally deducted pre-tax where permitted. Coverage tiers, tobacco surcharges, domestic-partner tax treatment, and wellness credits are described in the enrollment guide.

Employees must review the first two pay statements after coverage starts or changes. A missing deduction does not mean coverage is free, and an incorrect deduction does not alone establish coverage. Benefits and Payroll coordinate corrections. Retroactive deductions may be spread over multiple checks when allowed.

## Leave and changes in hours

Benefits may continue during paid leave if the employee pays the normal employee contribution. During unpaid leave, the employee may need to pay contributions directly. HR provides written instructions, due dates, and consequences of nonpayment. Protected leave laws and plan documents control continuation rights.

If scheduled hours fall below the eligibility threshold, coverage may continue through a measurement or stability period. Benefits will issue a notice before termination whenever practicable. Employees losing coverage may have continuation or marketplace rights and should follow the deadlines in the notice.

## Claims, privacy, and appeals

Insurance carriers and plan administrators decide claims under plan terms. Employees should use the member portal for network, prior authorization, and claim status questions. A manager cannot guarantee that a provider or treatment is covered.

An adverse benefit determination includes appeal instructions and deadlines. Employees may contact Benefits for navigation help, but Benefits does not replace the carrier's appeal. Medical information is restricted to personnel who need it for plan administration and is not placed in ordinary manager files.

## Separation and continuation

Active coverage generally ends on the last day of the month in which eligible employment ends, subject to the plan document and local requirements. The continuation administrator sends an election notice to the recorded address. Employees are responsible for keeping contact information current and meeting election and payment deadlines.

Life insurance conversion, health account use, and retirement plan rights have separate deadlines. Employees should not rely on this summary for a separation decision; Benefits will provide an individualized status notice.

## Controlling documents

Summary plan descriptions, insurance certificates, and formal plan documents control over this policy when there is a conflict. The company may amend or terminate plans as permitted by law. Benefits questions involving urgent treatment, disputed eligibility, or an approaching appeal deadline should be escalated immediately.

## Retirement plan

Eligible employees may join the company 401(k) plan on the first payday after 90 days of employment. The company matches 100 percent of the first 4 percent of eligible pay that the employee contributes. The match is calculated each pay period. Employee contributions vest immediately. The company match vests after three years of service, all at once. An employee who leaves before that date forfeits the unvested match. Loans and hardship withdrawals follow the plan document and are requested from the retirement recordkeeper, not from a manager.

The employee changes the contribution rate in the retirement portal. A change entered at least five business days before payday applies to that payday. The company does not recommend a contribution rate or an investment. Employees who want advice should use the recordkeeper's licensed adviser or their own adviser.

## Equity awards

Some offer letters include a discretionary equity award, described as a number of fictional demo units in the RAGs to Riches Demo Company equity plan. An award is not wages, is not guaranteed for future years, and has no value until the conditions in the award agreement are met. The standard vesting example in this demo is 25 percent after one year and the rest in equal monthly portions over the following three years, while the person remains an employee. A termination usually stops unvested units. The award agreement, not this summary, controls vesting, a change in control, and what happens at separation.

Employees can see granted, vested, and unvested units in the equity tile of the HR system. They do not trade or transfer demo units. Questions about taxes on an award go to a tax adviser. People Operations can explain what the screen shows, but cannot predict a future unit price or promise a refresh grant. A refresh grant, when offered, is a new award and is not part of the annual bonus.

## Health accounts, life insurance, and disability

Employees enrolled in the high-deductible medical option may contribute to a health savings account. The company contributes $500 per year for employee-only coverage and $1,000 per year for other coverage levels, deposited quarterly. Employees enrolled in the standard medical option may elect a health care flexible spending account during enrollment, up to the annual limit published in the enrollment guide. Up to $640 of an unused flexible spending balance may carry into the next year. Amounts above that are forfeited. A dependent-care flexible spending account is a separate election for eligible care of a dependent while the employee works.

The company pays for basic life insurance equal to one times annual base pay, and for short-term disability that replaces 60 percent of base pay after a seven-day waiting period, for up to 13 weeks, subject to the certificate. Long-term disability, supplemental life, and accidental death coverage are optional employee-paid elections. Disability claims are filed with the insurer. A manager is told only the approved dates away from work.

## Employee assistance program

Every regular employee and each person living in their household may use the employee assistance program from the hire date. The program includes up to six counseling sessions per issue per year, plus referrals for legal, financial, and childcare questions. Use is confidential. The company receives only aggregate usage counts, not names. Contact information is on the benefits tile in the HR system. Using the program is not reported to the manager and is not a performance issue.
