# Workplace Conduct and Respect Policy

> Synthetic training policy. Reporting channels and names are fictional.

## Standard of conduct

Employees must treat coworkers, applicants, customers, vendors, and visitors with professionalism and respect. The policy applies at offices, remote meetings, messaging systems, work travel, social events, and other situations connected to work.

Discrimination, harassment, sexual harassment, bullying, threats, retaliation, and deliberate exclusion based on a protected characteristic are prohibited. Protected characteristics vary by location and may include race, color, religion, creed, sex, pregnancy, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, national origin, ancestry, age, disability, medical condition, genetic information, veteran status, marital or family status, and other legally protected traits.

Conduct can violate this policy even if it does not violate law. Intent does not determine impact, and a person need not confront the subject of a report before seeking help.

## Examples

Prohibited conduct can include slurs, degrading jokes, unwanted sexual attention, repeated comments about a person's body or identity, sharing offensive images, stalking, threats, sabotaging work, or conditioning an employment benefit on personal or sexual conduct.

Reasonable performance feedback, respectful disagreement, and ordinary work direction are not harassment merely because they are unwelcome. They must still be delivered consistently and professionally. Concerns about management style or fairness may be reported and will be routed appropriately.

## Reporting

Anyone may report a concern to a manager, HR, the ethics mailbox, or the confidential reporting line. A manager who learns of possible discrimination, harassment, retaliation, violence, or other serious misconduct must notify HR within one business day and may not promise secrecy.

A report should include what happened, when and where it occurred, people involved, witnesses, and relevant records if known. Complete information is helpful but not required. Anonymous reports are accepted, although anonymity may limit follow-up.

An employee facing immediate danger should contact local emergency services and workplace security. Reports of threats or weapons are escalated under the violence prevention procedure.

## Response and investigation

HR assesses immediate safety, separation, reporting-line, evidence-preservation, and support needs. Interim measures may include schedule changes, remote work, reporting changes, no-contact direction, or paid leave. Interim measures are not findings and should avoid burdening the reporting person where practical.

Investigations are prompt, impartial, and appropriately documented. Participants are expected to cooperate honestly and preserve information. Knowingly false statements may lead to action, but an unsubstantiated good-faith report is not a false report.

Information is shared only with people who need it to assess, investigate, respond, or meet legal duties. Absolute confidentiality cannot be promised. Outcome information is provided to the extent appropriate and lawful.

## Retaliation

Retaliation is prohibited against anyone who raises a good-faith concern, seeks an accommodation, participates in an investigation, refuses discriminatory conduct, or exercises a workplace right. Retaliation can include termination, reduced opportunities, threats, social pressure, schedule manipulation, or unwarranted scrutiny.

Employees should report suspected retaliation promptly, even while another investigation is pending. Retaliation is reviewed as a separate violation.

## Corrective action

When a violation is found, the company takes action proportionate to severity, impact, intent, role, and history. Measures may include coaching, training, warning, reassignment, loss of supervisory duties, suspension, or termination. Vendors and visitors may lose access.

Managers must consult HR before taking action connected to a conduct report. Records are retained under the investigation and retention rules.

## Relationships and conflicts

Employees must disclose a romantic, family, household, or close financial relationship when one person can influence the other's pay, schedule, promotion, performance rating, investigation, or continued employment. HR may change reporting or decision responsibilities.

Disclosure is intended to manage conflicts and does not itself imply misconduct. Employees may not participate in an employment decision involving a close relation or partner.

## Speech and lawful rights

Nothing in this policy is intended to restrict lawful discussion of wages, hours, working conditions, protected concerted activity, whistleblowing, or reporting to a government agency. Employees do not need company permission to exercise those rights.

Questions about whether conduct is covered should be directed to HR. Uncertainty is not a reason to delay a safety concern or possible harassment report.
