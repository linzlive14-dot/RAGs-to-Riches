# Holidays and Civic Leave Policy

> Synthetic training policy. Holiday names and pay rules are fictional demonstration content.

## Observed company holidays

RAGs to Riches Demo Company observes these paid holidays for eligible employees: New Year's Day, Martin Luther King Jr. Day, Memorial Day, Juneteenth, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving Day, the Friday after Thanksgiving, and Christmas Day. When a listed holiday falls on a Saturday, the company observes it on the preceding Friday. When it falls on a Sunday, the company observes it on the following Monday. HR publishes the dated calendar for the next calendar year each November.

A holiday is a paid day off for regular full-time and part-time employees who are scheduled to work that day and who are in active paid status. Interns on the company payroll receive holiday pay for a holiday that falls on a scheduled workday. Contractors and agency workers follow their supplier's terms. An employee on unpaid leave does not receive holiday pay unless a written leave approval says otherwise.

Holiday pay for a full-time employee is the regular scheduled hours for that day, normally eight hours. Part-time holiday pay equals the hours the employee was scheduled to work. Holiday pay is not overtime by itself. Hours actually worked on the holiday are recorded separately.

## Working on a holiday

Some roles must provide coverage on an observed holiday, including customer support, security operations, and facilities. The manager posts the holiday roster at least 14 calendar days in advance when the need is known. An employee who works on an observed holiday records the hours worked and also receives either holiday pay or a substitute day off in the same pay period, as the manager and the employee agree in the HR system. A substitute day must be used within 90 calendar days. It is not paid out at separation unless local law requires payout of earned time.

Volunteering to cover a holiday does not change remote-work eligibility or the assigned work location. Employees may not work a holiday from an unapproved location.

## Floating holiday

Each regular employee receives one floating holiday per calendar year, in addition to the observed company holidays. The floating holiday is available on January 1, or on the hire date for someone who starts later in the year. An employee hired on or after October 1 receives the floating holiday only if they are still employed on January 1 of the next year; they do not receive a prorated floating holiday for the hire year.

The employee requests the floating holiday in the HR system at least five calendar days in advance. The manager approves it using the same staffing rules that apply to a one-day vacation request. The floating holiday does not carry into the next year and is not paid out if unused. It may be used for a religious observance, a cultural holiday, a school event, or any other personal reason. The employee does not need to state the reason.

## Bereavement

An employee may take paid bereavement leave after the death of a family member. For a spouse, domestic partner, child, stepchild, parent, stepparent, sibling, grandparent, grandchild, or parent-in-law, the employee may take up to five scheduled workdays. For an aunt, uncle, niece, nephew, cousin, or sibling-in-law, the employee may take up to three scheduled workdays. For a coworker, the manager may approve up to one scheduled workday so the employee can attend a service.

Bereavement days do not have to be consecutive, but they should be used within 30 calendar days of the death or of a memorial service, whichever is later. The employee tells the manager the relationship and the dates. HR may ask for a published obituary, service program, or similar confirmation only when there is a specific reason to verify the leave, and will not ask for medical information about the person who died. Additional unpaid days may be requested if travel is unusually long. Those extra days follow the personal-leave rules.

Bereavement pay is the employee's regular rate for the scheduled hours missed. It is not deducted from paid time off.

## Jury duty and court appearances

The company pays regular wages for jury duty for up to 10 scheduled workdays in a calendar year. The employee should give the manager a copy of the jury summons as soon as it arrives and should keep the manager informed of each day they must report. Jury-duty pay from the court may be kept by the employee; the company does not reduce wages by that amount during the paid period. After 10 paid days, further jury service is unpaid unless local law requires otherwise, and the employee may use paid time off if they choose.

An employee subpoenaed as a witness in a matter that is not related to company business receives the same pay treatment as jury duty. Time spent as a party in a personal lawsuit is not paid civic leave; the employee uses paid time off or an approved unpaid absence. Time spent assisting the company in a legal matter is work time.

## Voting and civic appointments

When an employee's work schedule does not leave two consecutive hours outside of working time while the polls are open, the company provides up to two hours of paid time to vote in a local, state, or federal election. The employee tells the manager at least two workdays in advance when the need is known. Proof of voting is not required. Time for a government appointment that cannot be scheduled outside work, such as a required immigration biometrics visit for the employee, may be paid for up to two hours with manager approval.

## Office closure and severe weather

Facilities announces an office closure, delayed opening, or early release through the company status page and the manager channel. Employees whose assigned office is closed, and who are not set up to work remotely that day, receive pay for the scheduled hours of the closure. Employees who are already approved to work remotely are expected to work unless the closure notice says otherwise or the employee has a safety, power, or caregiving barrier. In that case the employee notifies the manager and records paid time off or an approved weather exception.

If the office stays open and travel is difficult, the employee uses judgment about safety. A late arrival or a decision to work from an already approved home location is not an attendance violation when the employee notifies the manager. The company does not ask anyone to travel in dangerous conditions to prove attendance.

## Religious and cultural observances

An employee who needs time away for a religious or cultural observance should first use the floating holiday or paid time off. If those options do not meet the need, the employee may ask HR for a schedule adjustment, a shift swap, or unpaid time. The request does not require a statement of doctrine. HR handles the request confidentially and looks for a workable adjustment that does not create an undue hardship for the team. A manager must not deny the floating holiday or paid time off merely because the reason is religious.

## Volunteer time

Regular employees may use eight hours of paid volunteer time each calendar year for service with a school, a nonprofit, or a community organization. The time is requested in the HR system at least five calendar days ahead, with the organization name and the date. It is not deducted from paid time off. It does not carry over and is not paid out. Political campaigning and service during a personal vacation do not qualify. The manager approves the time when coverage allows. An employee who wants more than eight hours uses paid time off for the extra hours.

## Donating paid time off

An employee may donate accrued paid time off, in whole-day increments, to the company leave-share bank. Donations are voluntary and are not taken from an employee who is below one week of balance. Donated days become general bank days. The donor cannot choose the recipient. An employee who is on an approved unpaid leave and has used their own paid time off may ask HR to draw up to ten days from the bank in a calendar year. HR grants the draw based on the leave and the bank balance, not on popularity or tenure. Managers do not solicit donations for a named coworker. The bank cannot be used to extend employment or to cover a negative balance.
